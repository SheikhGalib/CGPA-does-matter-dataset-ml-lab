Problem statement

Two questions, one merged dataset

The course asks for a model. The data asks a harder question first — whether self-reported habits carry any signal at all, and whether two separately collected surveys can honestly be pooled to find out.

Pipeline A — Feature influence

Which of a student's habits, circumstances and attitudes are actually associated with academic performance? A ranking problem, answered with statistics rather than a black box.

Pipeline B — CGPA prediction

Given a student's answers, predict their CGPA band. A supervised classification problem over four ordered classes.

Two experiments, never merged
Primaryall features, including last semester's SGPA band
Behaviour-focusedthe same features, minus prior performance
The two are kept apart. Prior academic performance dominates any model it sits in, so the behaviour-only run is what actually answers Pipeline A.

Related work & base paper

Standing on Cortez & Silva

Cortez & Silva (2008) — UCI Student Performance

Predicted secondary-school grades for Portuguese students using demographic, social and school features gathered from reports and questionnaires. Compared Decision Tree, Random Forest, Neural Network and SVM. The benchmark dataset behind 200+ later studies.

~72%
reported four-class accuracy
33
attributes
G1, G2
strongest predictors — prior grades
Their headline caveat is that predictive power collapses once the prior-period grades are removed. That is the exact effect we set out to measure.
What we contribute
New primary data, and five times more of it
1,126 responses from four Bangladeshi universities — a different population, education system and grading culture, at a sample size that can actually detect a small effect.
A documented cross-survey merge
Two forms with different wording, different band schemes and a bilingual answer set, reconciled into one schema with the effect size of every difference measured.
A replication test, not just a result
Every association is re-run on each sample independently. Two findings from our own 230-response study did not replicate, and we retract them.
Multiple-comparison correction, applied honestly
15 features tested, Benjamini–Hochberg applied — and a bug in our own first implementation found and fixed.

Merging04 / 17
Step 1 — how two surveys became one dataset

Same questions, different dialect

Two Google Forms, run separately, asking the same 19 substantive questions in a different order and with different English. Merging them is four passes, each of which can silently destroy the dataset if skipped.

Source A — multi-university form

230 responses from BUET (104), BRAC (101) and CUET (25). English answer values. A department dropdown plus a separate “if not listed” write-in box. GPA collected in 6 bands. No zero option on the commitments question.

Source B — KUET form

896 responses, all KUET, so the form never asked for a university. Bilingual answer values (90% or more (৯০% বা তার বেশি)). One free-text department box. GPA in 4 bands, or type a number.

Four harmonisation passes
1 · Columns21 columns hand-mapped to one schema, with an assert
2 · ValuesStrip the Bangla bracket and emoji out of every answer value
3 · OptionsOverlap-check all 17 shared columns — 3 real mismatches found
4 · ConcatenateStack rows, keep source_form for auditing, exclude it from every model
Pass 1 rejects fuzzy string matching: a threshold loose enough to match “admitted into KUET” with “admitted into your university” also merges the two different satisfaction questions with each other.

Pass 2 is the one that makes the merge real. Without it 90% or more and 90% or more (৯০%…) are two unrelated categories, the 1,126 rows become two disjoint blocks sharing no feature values, and every model learns which form a row came from instead of anything about students.

Preprocessing08 / 17
Two decisions worth defending

An assumption, and a confounded variable

The merge turned an assumption into evidence

Our 230-response study had to assume that a blank on the commitments question meant “I have none”, because that form offered no zero option. The KUET form did offer None, to a comparable population answering the same question.

26.5% of KUET students actively pressed None; 19.1% of Source-A students left it blank when the button did not exist. Same behaviour, two expressions. This is the single clearest argument for having merged the datasets: an assumption became a measurement.

Confound — semester

“Semester 11” does not mean further along than “semester 7” — it means BRAC. A model given the raw number can recover the institution and learn per-university grading habits while appearing to learn about academic progress.

Replaced with academic_progress = semester ÷ programme length, comparable across all four institutions. Raw semester_raw never leaves the notebook.

EDA09 / 17
Exploratory data analysis

Is this one dataset, or two?

The merge holds — with one named exception

KUET students and BUET/BRAC/CUET students report study habits, sleep, stress, environment, support and distraction at close to the same rates (|δ| < 0.15). That is the empirical licence to pool them. The exception is admission_satisfaction (δ = −0.34).

Two readings we cannot separate: a real difference, or a response artefact — KUET's Very dissatisfied option drew 40% of answers while Dissatisfied drew 5%. We keep the feature and quarantine it: any finding resting on it is re-checked within source. It is also why source_form is excluded from every model.

What else the EDA settled

No degenerate feature — zero items exceed a 60% modal share, so nothing is dropped for being uninformative. No multicollinearity — no feature pair exceeds |r| = 0.7, so no feature needs removing and we are not forced into regularised models only.

Grading culture does differ by institution (χ² = 34.6, p = 7×10⁻⁵) but weakly: Cramér's V = 0.102. And sleep_duration is confirmed monotonic, not inverted-U — which retroactively justifies encoding it by duration.

Pipeline A — which features matter

Four methods. Three survivors.

No single importance measure is trustworthy on survey data, so we ran four and ranked by consensus — then corrected for having tested 15 features at once.

Spearman ρmonotonic rank association
Mutual informationcatches non-linear structure
Kruskal–Wallis Hdistribution differs across bands?
Permutation importancereal out-of-fold accuracy drop
Permutation importance, not the tree's built-in Gini importance — Gini is measured on training data and is biased toward features with many distinct values.
3 of 15 features survive FDR correction

desired_department_match ρ = +0.119, q = 0.001  ·  attendance ρ = +0.090, q = 0.020  ·  weekly_responsibilities ρ = −0.086, q = 0.020. The strongest behavioural correlate of CGPA is not a habit at all — it is whether the student got into the subject they wanted.

Both facts must be reported together: these effects are real (they survive correction on 1,108 students) and they are small (the largest explains ~1.4% of the variance). They are detectable only because n = 1,108 reaches |ρ| ≈ 0.059, where n = 230 needed |ρ| ≈ 0.130.

Feature influence11 / 17
The replication test

What the merge overturned

The most instructive result here is not a number — it is which of our own earlier findings survived a five-fold increase in sample size. We re-ran every test on each sample independently.

Two findings we retract

At n = 230 it looked like students who understood lectures better got worse grades (topic_clarity ρ = −0.161, the largest effect in that dataset) and that more stress went with better grades (ρ = +0.119).

Neither appears in the 896-student KUET sample (ρ = +0.053 and −0.016) and neither survives in the merged data. They were small-sample noise. We retract them — and this is exactly what merging the datasets was for.

Two findings that replicate

desired_department_match is significant in both samples independently (+0.144 at n = 230, +0.113 at n = 896). weekly_responsibilities shows the same sign and the same size in both (−0.080 and −0.089) and only becomes detectable when they are pooled.

Only 6 of 15 features even agree in sign across the two samples — about what chance predicts for effects this small, and a standing caution against reading anything into a single unreplicated survey correlation.

Why this protocol
Repeated stratified 10-fold CV
A single holdout swings several points on the seed alone. 30 fits per model make the ranking stable enough to act on; stratified so every fold keeps the 4-class balance.
Accuracy
Fair here only because the classes are balanced (21–29%).
Macro-F1 — what we rank by
Averages the four classes equally, so a weak class cannot hide behind a strong one.
Cohen's κ
Corrects for agreement expected by chance. The metric that exposes a model which is really ZeroR wearing a hat.
Within-1-band accuracy
The target is ordinal — predicting C2 when the truth is C3 is a smaller error than predicting C0.
Trap: ZeroR scores 77.6% on within-1-band — higher than any behaviour model — without learning anything, because it always predicts a middle class that is within one band of three of the four. Always read within-1-band next to κ, which is 0.000 for ZeroR by construction.

Results14 / 17
Reading the comparison honestly

A podium is not always a winner

Two things the ranking table does not say on its own — including the check the primary result had to pass before we were willing to quote it.

The top of each table is a statistical tie

The macro-F1 gap between 1st and 2nd on the behaviour experiment is 0.0049, against a fold-to-fold accuracy standard deviation of ±0.043.

So we do not crown a winner on that margin. SVM, Random Forest and Logistic Regression are indistinguishable here; so are Gradient Boosting, Random Forest and the Decision Tree in the primary run. Saying so is more useful than picking one and defending it in the viva.

Is the +12.2 lift real, or arithmetic?

For KUET rows the CGPA target includes the semester whose SGPA is the primary feature, so part of the lift could be definitional. Source A has no such overlap — its CGPA is measured strictly before that semester.

Run separately: +12.4 points on the KUET subset, +13.5 points on the no-overlap subset. The lift is if anything larger where the overlap cannot exist, so the primary result is prediction, not arithmetic, and needs no discounting.

Would more data help?

A 10× increase in training data bought about three accuracy points (0.270 at n = 99 → 0.300 at n = 997), and the curve is flat over its final third. The ceiling is the features, not the sample size.

What would raise accuracy

  • Registrar grades instead of self-reports.
  • Course-level, not semester-level, records.
  • Longitudinal data, so habit changes can be measured.
  • Prior performance — already worth +12.2 points.
  • Not more rows of the same questionnaire.

Verdict

An interpretable model is worse than guessing here. The depth-3 decision tree we would show a viva panel reaches 27.3% — below the 29.0% ZeroR floor. Interpretability and accuracy are in direct conflict on this data, and that conflict is a finding, not a bug.

What we did

And a bug we found in our own statistics. Our first Benjamini–Hochberg implementation took its running minimum in the wrong direction, collapsing every q-value onto the smallest and reporting 15 of 15 features as significant. Fixed in both notebooks, both re-run; the corrected count is 3 of 15. We report it because checking your own statistics is the point.

Conclusions17 / 17
Conclusions & limitations

What we can honestly claim

Merging worked — for the statistics
Five times the data turned a null result into three FDR-surviving associations, and falsified two of our own earlier findings.
Behaviour alone barely predicts grades
κ = 0.054 against a 29.0% floor; the numeric view gives R² = −0.001, no better than quoting the class average.
Prior performance carries the signal
One variable moves accuracy 31.2% → 43.4%. This replicates Cortez & Silva's caveat in a new population.
Limitations
  • Self-reported grades and habits — no registrar data to verify against.
  • Convenience sample: 79% of rows are KUET, and 75% of those sit in one semester.
  • The CGPA reference point differs by one semester between the two forms.
  • admission_satisfaction may carry a first-option response artefact on the KUET form.
  • GPA collected in bands, so no true continuous CGPA exists.
  • Cross-sectional — every result is an association, never a cause.
